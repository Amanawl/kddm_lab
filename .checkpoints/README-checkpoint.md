# Assignment 1: Short Note

`customer_id` is generated for every order, so it is unique in all 99,441 rows, while `customer_unique_id` identifies the actual person and repeats (96,096 distinct values, with 2,997 people holding more than one `customer_id`), so `customer_unique_id` is the column to use when counting customers. `order_items` is the junction table: it resolves the many-to-many relationships between orders and products and between orders and sellers. The circle between `orders` and `order_items` is justified by the data, because 775 orders have no item rows at all (603 unavailable, 164 canceled, and 8 in other statuses). The `customers` to `orders` line is stricter in the data than my diagram: I drew "one or many" orders per customer, but every `customer_id` has exactly one order (minimum and maximum of 1). I also left the circle off the product and seller sides because every product and seller has at least one item (minimums of 1).
